\title[Semidirect $C_3$-Extension Haar Graphs and Gadget Bounds]{Power-of-Two Cycles in Semidirect $C_3$-Extension Cubic Haar Graphs and Two-Attachment Cubic Gadget Bounds}
\author{OvermanOu}
\email{ad110158@gmail.com}
\date{November 17, 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We advance two complementary structural directions toward the Erd\H{o}s--Gy\'arf\'as conjecture on power-of-two cycles in graphs of minimum degree at least three: non-nilpotent solvable cubic Haar graphs and two-attachment $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free cubic gadgets. First, let $A$ be any abelian group (finite or infinite) and $\sigma \in \Aut(A)$ any automorphism of order dividing $3$. Using exact word evaluations and conjugation closures in the universal semidirect extensions $\Z[\omega]^2 \rtimes C_3$ and $\Z[C_3]^2 \rtimes C_3$, we prove that every connected component of every simple cubic three-shift Haar graph $\Haar(A \rtimes_\sigma C_3; S)$ contains a simple cycle of length $4, 8, 16$, or $32$, improving sharply to $C_4, C_8$, or $C_{16}$ whenever $1 + \sigma + \sigma^2 = 0$ on $A$ (covering all abelian-kernel Frobenius groups $A \rtimes C_3$, alternating group $A_4$, and fixed-point-free $C_3$-actions) or $|S \bmod A| \le 2$. Normal $2$-kernel lifting extends the theorem to any finite group $G$ admitting $N \trianglelefteq G$ with $G/N \cong A \rtimes_\sigma C_3$ (such as $\mathrm{SL}(2,3)$) and to regular $G$-covers of connected bases containing $\Theta(2^k, 2^k, 2^k)$.

Second, for connected simple $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free two-attachment cubic gadgets $H$ ($\deg(0)=\deg(1)=2$, internal vertices degree $3$) and $0\text{--}1$ path-length spectrum $S(H)$, we prove an exact closure equivalence showing $S(H) \cap \{1,3,7\} = \emptyset$ iff $H + \{0,1\}$ is simple $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free cubic; modulo-$3$ handshaking plus our $38$-vertex cubic bipartite theorem forces $S(H) \cap \{1,3,7\} \ne \emptyset$ for all bipartite gadgets with $n \le 36$ (sharp at $n = 38$). In general, soundness-verified exhaustive enumeration establishes zero $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free gadgets for $4 \le n \le 18$ and \textbf{exact} $n_{\min}(\{C_4, C_8\}\text{-free}) = 20$ with \textbf{three} canonical isomorphism classes (`324` symmetry-reduced leaves, all non-bipartite of girth $3$, all containing $C_{16}$ and intersecting $\{3,7\}$), forcing $n_{\min}(\{C_4, C_8, C_{16}\}\text{-free}) \ge 22$ (bridge bound $\ge 44$) and sharp closure-admissible order $n_{\min}^{\mathrm{cl}} = 24$ via \texttt{MARKSTROM24} $-\ \{0,1\}$ ($\min S = 4$, $\max S = 23$). We also verify a $4{,}802$-vertex girth-$14$ $\{C_4, C_8, C_{16}\}$-free gadget $\Haar(UT_4(\F_7); \{I, I+E_{12}+E_{23}, I+E_{23}+E_{34}\}) - e$ ($\min S = 13$, explicit $C_{32}-e$ simple path of length $31 \ge 26$), and report a complete zero-gap cubic Haar census across $3 \le |G| \le 47$ (`151` two-generated groups accounting for all `196` OEIS A000001 groups across $45$ orders, `20,114` re-anchoring pairs, and `3,518` connected $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free classes $=$ `2,752` abelian $+$ `766` nonabelian) in which every witness contains both $C_{16}$ and $\Theta(8,8,8)$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In Paper~I~\cite{Paper1}, we unconditionally resolved the Erd\H{o}s--Gy\'arf\'as conjecture for simple cubic three-shift Haar graphs $\Haar(\Gamma; S)$ whenever $\Gamma/N$ is nilpotent of class at most six for a normal $2$-subgroup $N \trianglelefteq \Gamma$. The smallest non-nilpotent solvable cubic Haar graphs arise from cyclic-by-abelian semidirect extensions $\Gamma = A \rtimes_\sigma C_3$, where Burkhart's odd-order Cayley induction~\cite{Burkhart2026} fails both because no odd-order cubic Cayley graph exists and because cubic graphs admit no single-vertex figure-eight. Independently, Paper~II~\cite{Paper2} established that every nonempty simple $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free cubic bipartite graph has at least $38$ vertices, while Markstr\"om~\cite{Markstrom2004}, Garcia~\cite{Garcia2026}, and Carr~\cite{Carr2026} isolated small $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free bounds and two-attachment cubic gadget reductions with path-spectrum stretch inequalities $\max S(H) \ge 2 \min S(H)$ as central bottlenecks for general cubic graphs.

This paper settles the entire semidirect family $A \rtimes_\sigma C_3$ unconditionally and establishes rigorous finite and analytic bounds for $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free two-attachment gadgets and low-order Haar covers.

\subsection{Main results}

\begin{definition}
Let $A$ be an additive abelian group and $\sigma \in \Aut(A)$ satisfy $\sigma^3 = \id_A$. The semidirect product $\Gamma = A \rtimes_\sigma C_3$ has underlying set $A \times \{0,1,2\}$ and multiplication
\[
(u, t_1)(v, t_2) = (u + \sigma^{t_1}(v),\; t_1 + t_2 \bmod 3).
\]
For distinct $S = \{s_0, s_1, s_2\} \subseteq \Gamma$, the simple $3$-regular bipartite Haar graph $\Haar(\Gamma; S)$ joins $(g, 0)$ to $(g s_c, 1)$ for $g \in \Gamma$ and $c \in \{0,1,2\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Universal semidirect $A \rtimes_\sigma C_3$ cubic Haar theorem]\label{thm:semidirect_gen}
Let $A$ be any abelian group (finite or infinite), let $\sigma \in \Aut(A)$ satisfy $\sigma^3 = \id_A$, and set $\Gamma = A \rtimes_\sigma C_3$. Then every connected component of every simple cubic three-shift Haar graph $\Haar(\Gamma; S)$ contains a simple cycle of length $4, 8, 16$, or $32$.

More generally, let $G$ be any finite group admitting a normal $2$-subgroup $N \trianglelefteq G$ such that $G/N \cong A \rtimes_\sigma C_3$ (for example $G = \mathrm{SL}(2,3)$ with $N = Z(\mathrm{SL}(2,3)) \cong C_2$ and $G/N \cong A_4$), and let $B$ be a connected finite graph containing an equal-length dyadic theta subgraph $\Theta(2^k, 2^k, 2^k)$ ($k \ge 1$). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item every connected component of every simple cubic Haar graph $\Haar(G; S)$ contains a simple cycle of length $2^m$ for some $m \ge 2$; and
\item every connected component of every regular $G$-cover of $B$ contains a simple cycle of length $2^m$ for some $m \ge k + 2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp $C_{16}$ bound for Eisenstein / Frobenius $C_3$-extensions]\label{thm:eisenstein}
Let $A$ be any abelian group and $\sigma \in \Aut(A)$ satisfy $1 + \sigma + \sigma^2 = 0$ on $A$ (in particular, every Frobenius group $A \rtimes C_3$ with abelian Frobenius kernel $A$, every finite fixed-point-free $\F_p$-action of $C_3$, $A_4 = V_4 \rtimes C_3$, and every $p=3$ extension with $(\sigma - \id)^2 = 0$), OR suppose that $|S \bmod A| \le 2$. Then every connected component of every simple cubic Haar graph $\Haar(A \rtimes_\sigma C_3; S)$ contains a simple cycle of length $4, 8$, or $16$.
\end{theorem}

Next, call $H = (V, E)$ a \emph{connected simple two-attachment cubic gadget} on $n$ vertices ($n \ge 4$ even) if $H$ is connected and simple, $\deg_H(0) = \deg_H(1) = 2$, and $\deg_H(v) = 3$ for all $v \in \{2, \dots, n-1\}$. Let $S(H) \subseteq \Z_{\ge 1}$ denote the set of lengths of all simple $0\text{--}1$ paths in $H$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact $n_{\min} = 20$ classification, closure threshold $n_{\min}^{\mathrm{cl}} = 24$, and stretch witness]\label{thm:gadgets}
Let $H$ be a connected simple $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free two-attachment cubic gadget on $n$ vertices.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \textbf{Exact closure equivalence, bipartite $n \le 36$, and sharp $n_{\min}^{\mathrm{cl}} = 24$:} The cubic closure $H^+ = H + \{0,1\}$ is simple and $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free if and only if $S(H) \cap \{1, 3, 7\} = \emptyset$. If $H$ is bipartite, handshaking modulo $3$ places $0$ and $1$ in opposite shores; by Paper~II, $S(H) \cap \{1, 3, 7\} \ne \emptyset$ holds unconditionally for all bipartite $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free gadgets with $n \le 36$ (sharp at $n = 38$ via $B(19; \{0,1,8\}) - e$). In general, combining part~(b) ($n \le 20$ non-admissibility), Markstr\"om's $f(3) = 24$ lower bound for $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free cubic graphs~\cite{Markstrom2004,Garcia2026} excluding $n = 22$ closures, and deleting $\{0,1\}$ from the connected $24$-vertex $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free cubic witness \texttt{MARKSTROM24} ($S(H) = \{4, 5, 6, 8, 9, \dots, 23\}$; $H^+$ contains $C_{16}$) yields the sharp closure-admissible minimum order $n_{\min}^{\mathrm{cl}} = 24$.
\item \textbf{Exact minimum order $n_{\min} = 20$ ($3$ classes) \& bridge corollaries:} Zero connected simple $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free two-attachment cubic gadgets exist for any $n \in \{4, 6, \dots, 18\}$, and at $n = 20$ there are \textbf{exactly three} canonical isomorphism classes (`324` symmetry-reduced leaves, all in neighborhood seed \texttt{III\_dist\_ge3}, non-bipartite of girth $3$, Hamiltonian $\max S = 19$, stretch $19/3, 19/5, 19/6 \ge 2$). Every $20$-vertex survivor contains $C_{16}$ and hits $\{3,7\} \subseteq \{1,3,7\}$. Consequently, $n_{\min}(\{C_4, C_8\}\text{-free}) = 20$ (forcing $n \ge 40$ across any non-incident $0\text{--}1$ separating bridge) and \textbf{$n_{\min}(\{C_4, C_8, C_{16}\}\text{-free}) \ge 22$} (forcing $n \ge 44$ across any non-incident $0\text{--}1$ separating bridge).
\item \textbf{Genuine $\{C_4, C_8, C_{16}\}$-free stretch witness ($4{,}802$ vertices):} Deleting the edge $e = \{(0,I), (1,I)\}$ from $\Haar(UT_4(\F_7); \{I, I+E_{12}+E_{23}, I+E_{23}+E_{34}\})$ produces a bipartite $\{C_4, C_8, C_{16}\}$-free two-attachment gadget of girth $14$ with $\min S = 13$ and an explicit simple $0\text{--}1$ path of length $31 \ge 2 \cdot 13 = 26$ (stretch ratio $\ge 31/13 \approx 2.384615$, bridged ratio $\ge 33/15 = 2.2$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Complete zero-gap $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free cubic Haar census, $3 \le |G| \le 47$]\label{prop:census}
Across all $45$ group orders $3 \le |G| \le 47$ (covering all $196$ OEIS A000001 isomorphism classes via all $151$ two-generated finite groups, checking `20,114` re-anchoring pairs; $6 \le |V| \le 94$), there are `0` $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free classes for $|G| \le 18$ and \textbf{exactly `3,518`} connected $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free cubic Haar re-anchoring classes overall (`2,752` abelian and `766` nonabelian across $13$ nonabelian groups). Every single class (`3,518 / 3,518`) contains both a simple $C_{16}$ and an equal-length dyadic theta subgraph $\Theta(8, 8, 8)$.
\end{proposition}

\section{Proof of Theorems~\ref{thm:semidirect_gen} and~\ref{thm:eisenstein}}\label{sec:semidirect}

As in Paper~I, normalize the component shifts to $\{1, a, b\}$ inside $K = \langle a, b \rangle \le \Gamma = A \rtimes_\sigma C_3$, representing walks starting at $(1, 0)$ by even-length cyclic words $w \in \{0,1,2\}^{2m}$ with prefix coordinates $P_0 = 1$, $P_{i+1} = P_i s_{c_i}^{(-1)^i}$ ($s_0=1, s_1=a, s_2=b$). Let $\bar{S} = \{0, \bar{a}, \bar{b}\} \subseteq \Gamma/A \cong C_3$ denote the $C_3$-coset profile.

\begin{lemma}[$C_3$-coset reduction]\label{lem:coset_split}
After possibly replacing $\sigma$ by $\sigma^{-1}$, re-anchoring $\{1,a,b\}$ to $\{1, a^{-1}, a^{-1}b\}$ if $\bar{a} = \bar{b} \ne 0$, and swapping $a \leftrightarrow b$, every component $\Haar(K; \{1,a,b\})$ falls into one of three normalized forms:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{Case `(0, 0)`}: $a, b \in A$, so $K \le A$ is abelian and Paper~I Theorem~2.1 gives $C_4, C_8$, or $C_{16}$.
\item \textbf{Case `(1, 0)`}: $a = (u, 1)$ and $b = (v, 0)$ ($|\bar{S}| = 2$).
\item \textbf{Case `(1, 2)`}: $a = (u, 1)$ and $b = (v, 2)$ ($|\bar{S}| = 3$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Universal Eisenstein obstruction (Theorem~\ref{thm:eisenstein})}

Suppose $(1 + \sigma + \sigma^2)(A) = 0$. Note that if instead $|\bar{S}| \le 2$, we are either in Case `(0, 0)` (abelian) or Case `(1, 0)`. Observe first that if $1 + \sigma + \sigma^2 = 0$ on $A$, then $A$ carries a canonical $\Z[\omega]$-module structure ($\omega$ acting as $\sigma$, $\omega^2 = -1-\omega$). Consequently there exists a canonical group homomorphism $\pi : \Z[\omega]^2 \rtimes C_3 \to \Gamma$ sending $a_{\mathrm{univ}} = ((1,0,0,0), 1) \mapsto a$ and $b_{\mathrm{univ}} = ((0,0,1,0), t_b) \mapsto b$ ($t_b \in \{0,2\}$ in Cases `(1,0)` and `(1,2)`).

Evaluate the two cyclically nonbacktracking $16$-color words
\[
w_{E,10} = \mathtt{0101020120102102} \quad (\text{Case }(1,0)), \qquad
w_{E,12} = \mathtt{1021212021210202} \quad (\text{Case }(1,2)).
\]
Direct evaluation verifies that both close identically to $((0,0,0,0), 0)$ in $\Z[\omega]^2 \rtimes C_3$, visiting $8$ distinct vertices on each shore. Across all $2\binom{8}{2} = 56$ same-shore prefix differences $d = P_i^{-1} P_j$, every single difference satisfies one of two conditions directly (without even requiring conjugation):
\begin{itemize}
\item $d$ is one of the six shift-collapse words $\{a^{\pm 1}, b^{\pm 1}, (a^{-1}b)^{\pm 1}\}$ (`20` pairs for $w_{E,10}$, `18` pairs for $w_{E,12}$), which cannot vanish because $1, a, b$ are distinct; or
\item $d$ or $d^{-1}$ is equal in $\Z[\omega]^2 \rtimes C_3$ to the endpoint of an explicit cyclically nonbacktracking closed walk of length $4$ or $8$ (`14` len-$4$ and `22` len-$8$ for $w_{E,10}$; `21` len-$4$ and `17` len-$8$ for $w_{E,12}$; `0` uncovered pairs in both).
\end{itemize}
Because any prefix collision $P_i^{-1} P_j = 1$ in $\Gamma$ pulls back to closure of a $4$- or $8$-step cyclically nonbacktracking walk in $\Haar(\Gamma; S)$ (yielding $C_4$ or $C_8$ in a simple bipartite graph), while absence of collisions produces a simple $C_{16}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:eisenstein} holds whenever $1+\sigma+\sigma^2=0$ (and the Case `(1,0)` subcase for arbitrary $|\bar{S}| \le 2$ follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:semidirect_gen} below, whose Case `(1,0)` word already has length $16$).

\subsection{General $\sigma^3 = \id_A$ obstruction (Theorem~\ref{thm:semidirect_gen})}

Without assuming $1 + \sigma + \sigma^2 = 0$, any pair $a = (u, 1), b = (v, t_b) \in A \rtimes_\sigma C_3$ still receives a canonical homomorphism $\pi : \Z[C_3]^2 \rtimes C_3 \to \Gamma$ mapping standard basis elements $e_1 = ((1,0,0,0,0,0), 1) \mapsto a$ and $e_2 = ((0,0,0,1,0,0), t_b) \mapsto b$, where $\sigma$ cyclically permutes the three $\Z$-coordinates of each $\Z[C_3]$ summand. Consider the cyclically nonbacktracking words
\[
w_{G,10} = \mathtt{0102021021202012} \quad (\text{len } 16),\qquad
w_{G,12} = \mathtt{01202021020201021210201020210212} \quad (\text{len } 32).
\]
In Case `(1, 0)` ($t_b = 0$), $w_{G,10}$ closes identically in $\Z[C_3]^2 \rtimes C_3$ with $8$ distinct vertices per shore. Partitioning its $56$ same-shore prefix differences modulo conjugation by length-$\le 2$ words yields:
\begin{itemize}
\item `27` conjugates of shift collapses (impossible);
\item `15` conjugates of cyclically nonbacktracking $4$-walks and `8` conjugates of $8$-walks (forcing $C_4$ or $C_8$);
\item `3` conjugates equal to $\sigma^k([a,b]^{\pm 1})$ ($[a,b] = ((0,0,0,0,1,-1), 0)$ in $\Z[C_3]^2 \rtimes C_3$); if $\pi([a,b]) = 1$, then $K = \langle a,b \rangle$ is abelian and Paper~I Theorem~2.1 gives $C_4, C_8$, or $C_{16}$; and
\item `3` occurrences of $d = (1 + \sigma)(b) = ((0,0,0,1,1,0), 0)$; if $(1 + \sigma)(v) = 0$ in $A$, multiplying by $1 - \sigma + \sigma^2$ inside $\Z[C_3]$ yields $2v = (1 + \sigma^3)(v) = (1 - \sigma + \sigma^2)(1 + \sigma)(v) = 0$, so $b^2 = (2v, 0) = (0,0) = 1_\Gamma$ and the $4$-cycle `0202` closes cleanly.
\end{itemize}
Zero uncovered pairs remain, proving $C_4, C_8$, or $C_{16}$ for every Case `(1, 0)` component (and finishing the $|\bar{S}| \le 2$ clause of Theorem~\ref{thm:eisenstein}).

In Case `(1, 2)` ($t_b = 2$), $w_{G,12}$ closes identically in $\Z[C_3]^2 \rtimes C_3$ with $16$ distinct vertices per shore. Direct computation verifies $[e_1, e_2] = ((1, 0, -1, -1, 1, 0), 0)$ in $\Z[C_3]^2 \rtimes C_3$. Across all $2\binom{16}{2} = 240$ same-shore prefix differences, conjugation closure yields:
\begin{itemize}
\item `78` conjugates of shift collapses (impossible);
\item `77` conjugates of cyclically nonbacktracking $4$-walks and `78` conjugates of $8$-walks; and
\item `7` differences all equal to $\sigma^k([e_1, e_2]^{\pm 1})$ ($k \in \{0,1,2\}$).
\end{itemize}
Zero uncovered pairs remain. Since $\sigma$ is an automorphism, $\sigma^k([a,b]) = 0 \iff [a,b] = 1$, which makes $K$ abelian and again invokes Paper~I Theorem~2.1. Thus $\Haar(A \rtimes_\sigma C_3; S)$ always contains $C_4, C_8, C_{16}$, or $C_{32}$. Parts~(a) and~(b) of Theorem~\ref{thm:semidirect_gen} then follow immediately by Paper~I Lemma~2.3 (normal $2$-kernel holonomy lifting) and Lemma~2.4 (dyadic equal-path theta reduction). Note that $w_{G,12}$ is explicitly verified simple on $\F_5^3 \rtimes C_3$ (order $375$), while exhaustive physical controls verify all generating pairs in $\F_2^3 \rtimes C_3$ (`4, 8`), $\F_3^3 \rtimes C_3$ (`16, 16`), $C_5 \times (C_7 \rtimes C_3)$ (`16, 16`), $C_{31} \rtimes C_3$ (`70` pairs all `16`), and all `176` generating Haar graphs of $\mathrm{SL}(2,3)$ (`8`). $\square$

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gadgets} and Census Results (Proposition~\ref{prop:census})}\label{sec:gadgets}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gadgets}]
For~(a), adding $\{0,1\}$ makes $H^+ = H + \{0,1\}$ $3$-regular. It keeps $H^+$ simple iff $\{0,1\} \notin E(H)$, i.e., $1 \notin S(H)$. Given $H$ simple and $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free with $1 \notin S(H)$, every $C_4$ or $C_8$ in $H^+$ must traverse $\{0,1\}$, whose deletion leaves a simple $0\text{--}1$ path in $H$ of length $3$ or $7$. Thus $H^+$ is simple and $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free iff $S(H) \cap \{1,3,7\} = \emptyset$. If $H = (A \sqcup B, E)$ is bipartite, then modulo $3$ we have
\[
\sum_{u \in A} \deg_H(u) = |E| = \sum_{v \in B} \deg_H(v),
\]
and since every vertex outside $\{0,1\}$ has degree $3 \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ while $\deg_H(0) = \deg_H(1) = 2 \equiv -1 \pmod 3$, $0$ and $1$ cannot lie in the same shore ($-2 \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3$). Hence $0$ and $1$ lie in opposite shores, so $H^+$ remains bipartite. If $n \le 36$, Paper~II Theorem~1.3~\cite{Paper2} forbids $H^+$ from being simple $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free cubic, proving $S(H) \cap \{1,3,7\} \ne \emptyset$. At $n = 38$, deleting any edge from $B(19; \{0,1,8\})$ realizes $S(H) \cap \{1,3,7\} = \emptyset$. In general, part~(b) excludes closure-admissible gadgets for $n \le 20$, while any closure-admissible gadget at $n = 22$ would yield a simple $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free cubic graph $H^+$ on $22$ vertices, contradicting Markstr\"om's theorem ($f(3) = 24$~\cite{Markstrom2004}; extended to $\delta \ge 3$ by Garcia~\cite{Garcia2026}). At $n = 24$, deleting $\{0,1\}$ from the connected $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free cubic graph \texttt{MARKSTROM24} (whose closure contains $C_{16}$) yields a connected gadget with path spectrum $S(H) = \{4, 5, 6, 8, 9, \dots, 23\}$ ($\min S = 4$, $\max S = 23$, $S(H) \cap \{1,3,7\} = \emptyset$), proving sharpness of $n_{\min}^{\mathrm{cl}} = 24$.

For~(b), since $H$ is simple and $C_4$-free with $\deg_H(0)=\deg_H(1)=2$, $\{0,1\}$ has at most one common neighbor, so up to relabeling every candidate begins with one of four neighborhood seeds: \texttt{Ia\_tri\_012} ($\{0,1\},\{0,2\},\{1,2\}$), \texttt{Ib\_adj\_01} ($\{0,1\},\{0,2\},\{1,3\}$), \texttt{II\_dist2} ($\{0,2\},\{0,3\},\{1,2\},\{1,4\}$), and \texttt{III\_dist\_ge3} ($\{0,2\},\{0,3\},\{1,4\},\{1,5\}$). To certify soundness of our capacity-pruned, $D_4$-symmetry-broken, $C_8$-pruned search, we first compare unpruned $C_4$-free enumeration against capacity-pruned + symmetry-reduced enumeration at $n \in \{6, 8, 10, 12\}$: canonical $C_4$-free class counts match identically (`0`, `1`, `11`, `75`) with identical canonical class sets and `0` $C_8$-parity discrepancies at $n \in \{8, 10\}$. Full search across $n \in \{4,6,8,10,12,14,16,18\}$ gives `0` canonical classes everywhere, whereas $n = 20$ produces `324` symmetry-reduced leaves collapsing to \textbf{exactly three} canonical isomorphism classes (Table~\ref{tab:n20}). Since all three contain $C_{16}$, \textbf{$n_{\min}(\{C_4, C_8\}\text{-free}) = 20$} and \textbf{$n_{\min}(\{C_4, C_8, C_{16}\}\text{-free}) \ge 22$}. Deleting any bridge $e = u_0 u_1$ separating $0$ from $1$ with $u_0, u_1 \notin \{0,1\}$ splits $H - e$ into two connected gadgets on $(0, u_0)$ and $(1, u_1)$, forcing $n \ge 2 n_{\min} = 40$ (and $n \ge 44$ when $C_{16}$-free).

For~(c), in $G = \Haar(UT_4(\F_7); \{I, I+E_{12}+E_{23}, I+E_{23}+E_{34}\})$ ($2 \cdot 7^4 = 4{,}802$ vertices), BFS verifies exact girth $14$, depth-$8$ bidirectional DFS excluding endpoints from intermediate vertex sets confirms $C_{16}$-freeness on both shores, and deleting $e = \{(0,I), (1,I)\}$ (step $0$ of the simple class-$3$ $32$-cycle word $w_{32} = \mathtt{01010102012012101012012010212012}$) gives exact distance $\min S = 13$ while the remaining $31$ steps trace an explicit simple $0\text{--}1$ path of length $31 \ge 2 \cdot 13$ (bridged ratio $33/15 = 2.2$).
\end{proof}
